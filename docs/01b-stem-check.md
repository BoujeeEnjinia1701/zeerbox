# ZeerBox STEM check

Verdict: the working principle of the existing design (option A, forced air through a wetted cellulose pad into an insulated store, with a controller that reads the weather) sits within known physics, chemistry and biology, and so do the principles of options B and C. Of the 37 claims for option A, 30 are within known science and 7 are plausible. Produce transpiration (S26) is closed by the calculation's scenario range; the other six need supplier data, field data or a decision by Amish: the pad's effectiveness and pressure drop (S3, S5), the shelf-life gain (S25), the battery's charging temperature (S31, ZBX-DEC-001 D6), the roof-space temperature (S35) and ventilation through a long sunless spell (S36, D3). Two details failed and were fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23: the 1.5 mm² load cable was protected only by the 15 A battery fuse (S16, D1), and, found by the independent review, the 4 L sump bleed let the pad water reach about 9 cycles of concentration (S37, D5). No row fails. Because six rows can only be closed by data or a decision, `stem_check` is **open**; under STANDARDS section 20 the repo keeps TRL 3 while they are open. Method: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which imports the calculation note's script `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py` (ZBX-CAL-001 v0.4) and writes `docs/04-calcs/stem_results.csv`.

The numbers checked are those of ZBX-CAL-001; each check uses a method independent of the one CAL used where one exists (an empirical wet-bulb fit, a Magnus moisture balance, an energy balance on the airflow), and otherwise a limit the claim must sit inside (the wet bulb, the free-air flow of the fans, the electrical input, the Shockley-Queisser limit). Several of option A's headline rows (S6 to S9) rest on the pad effectiveness of S3: the 8 K of PR-002 holds down to about 68 % effectiveness.

## Proposed, awaiting Amish

- **S36, no-power ventilation (ZBX-DEC-001 D3).** The battery runs hold-mode ventilation and the controller for about 40 h with no sun. Beyond that the fans stop and, with the door shut, the room is sealed: 480 kg of produce respiring at about 50 mg CO₂/(kg·h) takes the air to about 2.3 % CO₂ in 12 h. The independent review added that in hold mode one fan at 30 % (about 5 Pa) can be stalled by a 3 m/s wind on the pad wall. Recommended: an always-open meshed vent and stack with a turbine ventilator head (the graft from options B and C). It lets some cooled air out on design days, so it is a safety trade-off for Amish.
- **S31, battery charging temperature (D6).** Recommended: move the power box into the store, where the air is about 24 °C.
- **S17, S18, S20, design gust and pressure zones (D7).** Recommended: keep 30 m/s until the site's design gust is known, with a hold point before the roof is built.
- **S27, sump disinfection (D8).** Recommended: a weekly shock dose of chlorine after cleaning.

## Fields not relevant

None. All five fields are touched: physics (thermodynamics, heat transfer, fluid mechanics, structures, circuits, optics of the panel), chemistry (battery, materials, corrosion, scale), biology (produce respiration and transpiration, microbiology of the sump, human physiology in a closed room), mathematics (units, scenario bracketing) and engineering (power budget, sensing, standards practice). Within physics, nuclear and ionising radiation and acoustics are not relevant: nothing in the design emits radiation, and the 12 W fans are not a noise hazard at the 2.5 m between the fan box and a person outside.

## Concept options

- **Option A, forced-air pad store (the existing design):** within known science; 7 plausible rows, one closed by the scenario range and six needing data or a decision; two detail fixes made (D1, D5).
- **Option B, passive wet-wall chamber:** within known science on cooling, water and ventilation (B1 to B3); a full room with 480 kg of produce and the day's warm field produce is held only about 3 K below outside air at the design point (first-order balance; ZECCs with lighter loads show 4 to 7 K), below the 8 K of PR-002; its stack flow drains cool air out of the low vent and draws 35 °C air in at the top. Its humidity (B4, 55 to 90 % RH depending on how wet the inner leaf runs) and mold in a damp, slowly ventilated room (B5) are plausible.
- **Option C, charcoal-wall wind cooler:** about 7 K in a light wind (C1), conditional on the turbine ventilator's airflow, for which no rated figure was found (C2), so both stay plausible; water use (C3) is within known science; the galvanised mesh in permanently wet charcoal (C4) and mold in the charcoal (C5) are plausible risks to confirm.

No option has a Fails row, so none is dropped.
